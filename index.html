The dough

One dough. A thousand pulls.

Every laghman noodle starts as one piece of dough. We stretch it when you order, not before.

01

Flour, water, salt.

Laghman is not a shape you buy — it's a motion you learn. Three things you have at home; the years of practice, you don't.

02

Pulled when you order.

A dough master swings the rope, folds it, and slaps it against the table — that crack you hear is your order being made.

03

Straight to the wok.

Seconds off the hook, it meets the wok or the broth — the loudest, shortest part of its life.

04

To your table, steaming.

The bowl lands under its own cloud. That's the whole secret. There isn't another one.

Our story

Built by a chef-author.

Laghman Express began with a conviction: that hand-pulled noodles from Central Asia belong on New York's map — made with the same care on 20th Avenue as anywhere the dish is loved.

Founder and chef-author Gulnoza Jurayeva built the brand around the dough itself — Kyrgyz, Uyghur, Uzbek, and Kazakh influences, cooked for the neighborhoods outside the door.

First 20th Avenue in Bensonhurst. Then Coney Island Avenue. Then Alpharetta, Georgia. Somewhere in between, The New York Times put us on its list of the city's 100 best — in 2024, and again in 2025.

“We do not simply serve food quickly. We serve culture with precision and dignity.”

The table

There is always tea.

Plates come and go; the teapot stays put. Ask for a pot when you order — and baursak to go with it. Fried dough has never met a tea it didn't like.
