About us

Built around noodles pulled by hand.

Chef Gulnoza Jurayeva founded Laghman Express as a Central Asian restaurant brand in New York. A trained team follows clear quality standards, moving quickly without sacrificing the care at the center of each bowl — work recognised by The New York Times in 2024, 2025 and 2026.

01

Pulled to order

A rope of dough gets swung, folded and slapped against the table until it is noodles. That crack you hear from the kitchen is your order being made.

02

Shaped by hand

Manty and samsa are closed one at a time, by the same hands, all day. Nothing about that step has been made faster in a hundred years.

03

Straight to the fire

Seconds off the hook, the noodles meet the wok. It is the loudest and the shortest part of their life.

04

To the table, steaming

The bowl lands under its own cloud. That is the whole secret. There isn't another one.

The room

Somebody's grandmother would recognise this table.

Laghman Express began with a conviction: that hand-pulled noodles from Central Asia belong on New York's map — made with the same care on 20th Avenue as anywhere the dish is loved.

Founder and chef-author Gulnoza Jurayeva built the brand around the dough itself — Kyrgyz, Uyghur, Uzbek and Kazakh influences, cooked for the neighbourhoods outside the door.

From 20th Avenue in Bensonhurst to Coney Island Avenue, Emmons Avenue and Alpharetta, the team carries the same standards into every kitchen. The New York Times named Laghman Express among the city's 100 best in 2024, 2025 and 2026.
